**Two feature claims of ours are narrowed by that sweep, and one correction.** `snow_leo_data/product-recalls-scraper` advertises 127,501 records across five US feeds in a 53-field schema and states that it pages openFDA **by cursor to read the whole archive** rather than stopping at openFDA's 25,000-record `skip` ceiling — so our 50,000-row ceiling is a per-run cap we should not describe as the widest in the niche, and a buyer who needs the full historical archive in one dataset has a cheaper option than us. `datalayer/fda-recall-enforcement` classifies openFDA's free-text `reason_for_recall` into root causes (undeclared allergen, sterility failure, nitrosamine impurity, software defect, component recall) and scores repeat-offender firms — at a lower price than ours. **The root-cause half of that gap is closed as of 2026-10-26: `rootCause`/`rootCauses` above classify the same free-text field into 19 documented categories (96.3% of rows on a holdout sample), and unlike their version the whole rule set is published, not a black box.** **The repeat-offender half closed the same way as of 2026-10-26, and simpler than first assumed — it does not need a cross-run history of our own (the "storage-design task" earlier notes here expected): `firmRecallCount` (optional, `includeFirmHistory`, off by default) reads openFDA's OWN all-time per-firm total directly, one extra query per unique firm in the run. Our `riskScore` grades severity/recency/scope; `rootCause` now parses *why* the recall happened, and `firmRecallCount` says how often this exact firm has done it before.
